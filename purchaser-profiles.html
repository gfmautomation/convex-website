Home/Purchaser lens packs

A contract review that knows who is buying

A periodic tenancy is a problem for a family moving in and an asset for an investor. A minimum lot size is irrelevant to one buyer and decisive for another. Convex lens packs frame the review and the letter for the client in front of you.

How a lens works

Chosen once, at the start

  • Every clause is still reviewed: the lens changes emphasis, never coverage.
  • The rating fits the buyer: the same clause can be green for one client and red for another.
  • The letter speaks to the client: explanations are written for that buyer's situation, not a generic purchaser.
  • Development is a separate switch: any profile can be combined with an intention to develop, subdivide or rebuild.
  • Not sure yet is an option: if the buyer's plans are not settled, Convex applies a balanced default.
Same clause

"The purchaser takes title subject to the existing periodic tenancy."

  • Owner-occupier: Red. The client cannot move in on settlement. Vacant possession is not being given, and ending the tenancy depends on the notice rules for the tenant.
  • Investor: Green. An income-producing tenancy continues from day 1. The letter confirms the rent, bond transfer and adjustments instead.
Same certificate

Planning certificate shows zone R2 Low Density Residential with a minimum lot size of 450 m².

  • Not developing: Green. Noted for completeness. Nothing in the zoning restricts ordinary residential use.
  • Intending to develop: Amber. On a 600 m² lot the minimum lot size prevents a Torrens subdivision into 2 lots. The letter explains the constraint so the client can take planning advice before exchange.

When the buyer's plans are not settled

Purchaser: not sure yet

Clients who have not decided whether they will live in the property or rent it out, or who are still deciding how to hold it.

Convex applies a balanced default. The letter is framed for a general purchaser, and items whose significance depends on the buyer's plans are flagged for you to confirm.

Develop: not sure yet

Questions

About purchaser lenses

Does a lens hide risks from the review?
No. Every clause and document is reviewed under every lens. The lens changes how each item is rated for the buyer and how the letter explains it.
What if I do not know the buyer's plans yet?
Use the Not sure yet option. Convex applies a balanced default: the review is framed for a general purchaser, and anything whose significance depends on how the buyer will use the property is flagged so you can confirm it with the client. If their plans become clear later, run the contract again under the right profile.
Can I use more than one lens on the same contract?
Select the profile that matches your client when you upload. If the client's plans change, for example they decide to develop, run the contract again under the new profile.
Does the SMSF lens give superannuation advice?
No. It frames the contract review for a fund buyer. Compliance with superannuation law remains a matter for the fund's accountant or licensed adviser.
Does the first home buyer lens work out duty concessions?
No. The lens explains the contract in plainer terms. Eligibility for duty concessions and grants depends on the buyer's circumstances and should be checked separately.
Do lenses apply to commercial contracts?
Yes. An owner-occupier buying premises for their own business, an investor buying a tenanted asset, an SMSF buying business real property and a developer buying a site each receive a commercial review framed for them.

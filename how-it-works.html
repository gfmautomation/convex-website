How a Convex review works

A Convex review follows the same order you would: set up the matter, understand the buyer, read every document in the contract against their circumstances, then put the findings into a letter ready to send.

  1. Enter the matter details

    Add the matter number and your client's names. These populate your letterhead and the letter itself. Use the same matter number as your practice management system so the letter files against the right matter.

    Once the Smokeball and Clio integrations are released Coming soon, the matter number and client details will be pulled through from the matter automatically.

  2. Choose the purchaser lens

    Tell Convex who is buying and what they intend to do:

    • Owner-occupier: upgrading or moving, downsizing, or a first home buyer.
    • Investor: buying in their own name, through a trust, or through an SMSF.
    • Development: whether the client intends to develop, subdivide or rebuild.
    • Not sure yet: if the client has not decided, Convex applies a balanced default and flags the items worth confirming with them.

    The letter is framed for that buyer. Nothing is left out of the review. More about purchaser lenses.

  3. Upload the contract

    Upload the contract PDF as you received it, with its annexures. Scanned pages are read with OCR. Several contracts can be uploaded at once on the Medium and Large plans.

  4. Convex reviews the whole contract

    Convex recognises the type of contract it is reading and reviews the contract together with every document attached to it, including the title, plans, planning certificate and prescribed documents. Each finding is scored for the buyer you selected.

  5. Review, download and send

    Within 3 to 5 minutes you have a traffic light report with the reds first, and a client-ready letter on your letterhead that explains every issue in plain English. Review it, make sure you are happy with it, and send it to your client with minimal edits.

    Download the letter in Word and PDF and save it to the matter in your practice management system.

The traffic light scale

What green, amber and red mean

Green

Standard or low-risk for this buyer. Included in the letter for completeness.

Amber

Needs explaining to the client, or may need a question to the vendor before exchange.

Red

Shifts risk or cost to the buyer, or is unusual enough that it typically warrants negotiation or amendment before exchange.

Scores reflect the purchaser lens you select. A clause can be green for one buyer and red for another.

Questions

About the process

What should I upload?
The contract for sale as issued by the vendor's solicitor or agent, with its annexures: special conditions, title search, plans, s10.7 planning certificate, sewer diagram, 88B instrument, strata or off-the-plan documents and any tenancy agreement or lease. Convex reviews everything attached.
Why do I enter the matter number and client names?
They populate the letterhead and the letter, so the output is ready to send. Use the same matter number as your practice management system so the letter files against the right matter. Once the Smokeball and Clio integrations are live, these details will be pulled through automatically.
Does Convex work out the contract type itself?
Yes. Convex recognises the type of contract it is reading. If it cannot, it tells you.
What if Convex is unsure about a clause?
It flags the clause for your review and says why.
How do I get the letter to my client?
Download the letter in Word or PDF and send it the way you usually do. Save a copy to the matter in your practice management system. The Smokeball and Clio integrations will save it back to the matter automatically once they are released.
